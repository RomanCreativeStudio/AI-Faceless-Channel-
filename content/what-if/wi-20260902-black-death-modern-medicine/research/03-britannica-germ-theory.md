## Relevant evidence

- Germ theory was developed in the mid-to-late 19th century, chiefly by
  Louis Pasteur (French chemist), Joseph Lister (English surgeon), and
  Robert Koch (German physician).
- Pasteur (mid-1800s) showed fermentation/putrefaction are caused by
  microorganisms, disproving spontaneous generation.
- Lister (1860s) applied germ theory to surgery (carbolic acid) to reduce
  infection.
- Koch (1880s) identified the pathogens causing tuberculosis and cholera
  and formulated Koch's postulates, the criteria for establishing that a
  specific microorganism causes a specific disease.
- Before germ theory, the prevailing explanatory model was spontaneous
  generation — disease and decay were believed to arise spontaneously
  rather than from living organisms. Germ theory replaced this and
  underlies modern vaccination, sanitation, and infection control.

## Researcher notes

This is the key source establishing that 14th-century Europe had no
concept of microorganisms causing disease — germ theory did not exist
until roughly 500 years after the Black Death. This is what makes the
"selected modern medical knowledge" premise a genuine hypothetical rather
than a small extrapolation.
