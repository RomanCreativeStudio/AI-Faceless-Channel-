## Relevant evidence

- Plague is caused by the bacterium *Yersinia pestis*, a zoonotic bacteria
  usually found in small mammals and their fleas.
- Transmission occurs via infected flea bites, unprotected contact with
  contaminated bodily fluids/materials, or inhalation of respiratory
  droplets from a pneumonic plague patient.
- Bubonic plague (swollen lymph nodes/"buboes") is the most common form;
  pneumonic plague affects the lungs and is the most severe, transmissible
  person-to-person via respiratory droplets.
- Without treatment, bubonic plague kills an estimated 30–60% of infected
  people; pneumonic plague is "invariably fatal" untreated, but shows high
  recovery rates if diagnosed and treated within 24 hours of onset.
- Modern treatment relies on antibiotics plus supportive care; antibiotics
  effectively eliminate the bacterium when given promptly, including for
  severe forms.
